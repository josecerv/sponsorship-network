## ENDORSER (Stage 2)

### E-R4 — intro + task + slider  (was ~200 words, now ~110)
You are an endorser for [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex].
Each candidate set has two candidates: one from [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex] (your organization's candidate) and one from [ORGANIZATION 2: Beacon]. You will see both candidates' scores on professional ability tests.
Your task is to set your endorsement strength for your organization's candidate. You are not choosing between the two candidates: you always endorse the [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex] candidate. Base your strength on how likely your candidate is to beat the other candidate on a separate logical reasoning test.
The slider runs 0 to 100. Higher = a stronger endorsement: more confident, more behind your candidate. Lower = a weaker endorsement: less confident, less at risk.
Evaluators will see your endorsement as [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex] backing its own candidate.

### E-R5 — who sees your endorsements  (was ~95, now ~53)
Evaluators (participants in a separate study) review your endorsements. For each one, they see: your organization, the candidate you endorsed (your organization's), and your endorsement strength.
They then decide what percent of a $0.50 bank (0-100%) to wager on you being right, and how much future review to allocate to you and your organization.

### E-R6 — earnings  (was ~250, now ~110)
Earnings: $1 for completing the study, plus a bonus of up to $3.
One endorsement (and the evaluator who reviewed it) is picked at random. Your bonus for it has three parts:
1. Candidate performance. Start with a $0.50 bank; your endorsement strength works like a wager.
   Win: $0.50 + ($0.50 x strength%)
   Lose: $0.50 - ($0.50 x strength%)
   At strength 80: $0.90 if you win, $0.10 if you lose.
2. Evaluator trust. If you win, add $1.00 x the evaluator's wager%.
3. Evaluator allocations. The evaluator gives 0-10 slots to you and 0-10 to your organization. Each slot = $0.05, up to $1.00 total, paid win or lose.

### E-R7 — comprehension checks: UNCHANGED (already terse; keeps options + answer key)

### E-R8 — who the candidates are  (was ~200, now ~110)
Who are the candidates?
The candidates are earlier participants who completed three tasks: a logical reasoning test, a general knowledge quiz, and a word search puzzle. They were paid for correct answers, so they had a real incentive to do well.
Each candidate's profile shows:
- Percentile scores on the quiz and word search (75th percentile = beat 75% of participants). "Unknown" = no score for that task.
- Organization: [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex] = your candidate; [ORGANIZATION 2: Beacon] = the other candidate.
- ID only, no names or photos.
Base your endorsement strength on the quiz and word search scores: how strongly you back your candidate to beat the other organization's candidate on the logical reasoning task.

### E-R10 — objective reminder  (was ~130, now ~78)
Set your endorsement strength: how confident you are that your organization's candidate will beat the other candidate on the logical reasoning task, and how much you want to put behind them.
- High strength = strong public backing: earns more if you are right, costs more if you are wrong.
- Low strength = safer: earns less if you are right, but risks less if you are wrong.
You always endorse the [ORGANIZATION 1: Vertex] candidate. You are only choosing how much to put behind that candidate.

## EVALUATOR (Stage 3)

### V-R4 — fielded opening  (was 26, now 24)
You will review real endorsement decisions made by another participant. Your decisions are real: they affect your bonus and can affect that participant's pay.

### V-R5 — framing intro  (was ~55, now ~29)
We study how people evaluate other people's decisions. You will see information about another participant's real decisions and decide how much of your own compensation to wager on them.

### V-R6 — setup  (was ~230, now ~125)
Before you wager, here is the setup.
Candidates: earlier participants who did three tasks: a word-search puzzle, a general-knowledge quiz, and a logical-reasoning test.
Endorsers: separate participants, each assigned to represent an organization. Each decision showed an endorser two candidates (their own organization's and another organization's), plus only the quiz and word-search scores. The endorser used a slider to show how confident they were that their candidate would beat the other candidate in the same set on the logical-reasoning test.
Your task: judge how much to trust one endorser. You will see a subset of their real decisions (order may vary), each showing their organization, the candidate endorsed (always their own organization's), and their confidence, but not performance data. You then wager part of your pay on whether they were right.

### V-R7 — endorser incentives telegraph  (was ~140, now ~97)
Endorsers had real incentives to endorse carefully: their pay works like a wager. A stronger endorsement earns more if their candidate wins (scores higher on the logical-reasoning test) and earns less if their candidate loses.
Their pay also depends on you:
- If their candidate wins: the more you wager, the bigger their bonus.
- At the end, you allocate review slots to this endorser and their organization; each slot adds to their bonus, win or lose.
One endorsement and one evaluator are picked at random for payment. If yours is picked, your wagers and allocations directly affect this endorser's pay.

### V-R8 — wager mechanics + rounds  (was ~170, now ~102)
You will evaluate three decisions from the same endorser, each a new, independent candidate set.
Each decision: start with a $0.50 bank and decide what percent of it (0-100%) to wager on the endorser being correct.
- Right: $0.50 + your wager
- Wrong: $0.50 - your wager
Example, 10% wager ($0.05): right = $0.55, wrong = $0.45.
0% = keep your $0.50 regardless. 100% = $1.00 or $0.00.
Correct means the endorsed candidate scored higher on the logical-reasoning test than the other organization's candidate in the same set. Total bonus = the sum of all three decisions (up to $3.00). You will see the result right after each wager.
