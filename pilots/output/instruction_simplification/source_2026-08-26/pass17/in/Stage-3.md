# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

You are an independent reviewer and belong to no organization.

You will review decisions made in an earlier study by another participant, the representative, on behalf of [Atlas/Vertex]. Each decision compared an [Atlas/Vertex] candidate with one from another organization. The representative decided how much of **$0.50** to place on the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate having scored higher on logical reasoning. They knew independent reviewers would see some decisions, and the more they placed, the more of their own pay rode on the candidate. They saw scores on two other tasks, when available, but not logical reasoning.

For each decision, you will see the representative’s information, the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate’s ID, and the amount placed. With each review’s **$0.50**, your job is to decide how far to follow the representative’s call.

---

# PAGE 2: MAKING YOUR REVIEWS

For each review, you receive a new **$0.50**. A slider from **0% to 100%** sets how much of it you place on the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate. If the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.50** plus the amount you placed; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.50** minus the amount you placed. The screen shows what you would receive under both outcomes. Place the amount that best reflects your judgment.

After each review, you learn which candidate scored higher and what you earned. You keep what you earn on every review. These earnings are paid as a bonus on top of your **[$X.XX]** participation payment.

After your reviews with this representative, you will make one choice about your final review, explained when you reach it, and after that review you will answer two questions about this representative and [Atlas/Vertex]. Your placements and your answers may also add to the representative’s bonus; the choice does not. Your own earnings always come from your reviews and from that final choice.

# PAGE 6B: CHOOSE YOUR FINAL REPRESENTATIVE

Your final review can come from this same representative or from a different representative, drawn at random. You choose how likely it is to be this representative: **0%** means it will certainly be a different one, and **100%** means it will certainly be this one.

You receive **$0.15** for this page. If you leave the slider at **50%**, a coin flip decides and the page costs you nothing, so you keep the whole **$0.15**. The further you move the slider from **50%**, the more it costs, and whatever you do not spend is yours.

- **50%**: **$0.00**
- **60%** or **40%**: **$0.01**
- **70%** or **30%**: **$0.03**
- **80%** or **20%**: **$0.06**
- **90%** or **10%**: **$0.10**
- **100%** or **0%**: **$0.15**

The screen shows the cost of the setting you pick.

The computer then makes one random draw, using the chance you set, and that decides who your final review comes from. This choice does not affect the representative’s bonus.

**You set the slider to 70%. What happens, and what do you keep?**

**B.** You spend **$0.15** and get this representative for sure.
**C.** Nothing changes unless you move the slider to **100%**.
