# A. SERVES THE ASK?

Onboarding and early scrutiny: Yes. “This study is set up like a staffing agency with two organizations, Vertex and Atlas, and you have joined [Vertex/Atlas] as a representative” establishes the world; “Your decisions will be seen: in a separate study, independent reviewers will see some of your referrals” brings scrutiny forward. Only Stage 3 says reviewers belong to neither organization.

Referral confidence and preferences: Mostly. “The amount you place shows how strongly you vouch for the [Vertex/Atlas] candidate” provides the framing, and the later-choice sentence signals evaluation. The draft still presents hoped-for reviewer reactions as facts.

Paid ask: Yes. “You receive $0.15 for this choice and keep whatever the choice does not cost” makes the chosen-chance mechanism costly, and a random draw implements it. “Affects only” still conceals the effect on how much the reviewer keeps.

# B. TRUTHFULNESS

- “Your decisions shape the view they form of you and of [Vertex/Atlas].” Behavioral effect asserted. Use: “Reviewers will see your decisions before evaluating you and, when relevant, choosing an organization.”
- “Independent reviewers will see it as your confidence in the referral.” Interpretation is not guaranteed. Use: “The amount represents your confidence in the referral.”
- “The more you place… makes the signal credible.” Credibility is assumed. Use: “The more you place, the more of your pay rises or falls with the candidate’s result.”
- “Being right pays you directly… and makes reviewers more willing to follow your next referral and to choose you again.” Only a randomly picked decision pays, and willingness is not guaranteed. Use: “If this decision is picked for your bonus, being right increases your payment in proportion to how much you placed. Reviewers learn the result before later decisions.”
- “The second part comes from… how much… they were willing to place… and how much more… they would want to review.” The placement is win-gated, and the answer pays $0.10 per point. Use: “The second part depends on the reviewer’s placement when your candidate wins and their answer about how much more of your work they would want to review.”
- “At the end, you will make one choice… and answer one question…” The final review separates them. Use: “After your reviews with this representative, you will make one choice about your final review. After that review, you will answer one question.”
- “Your answer and your decisions may also add to the representative’s bonus.” “Decisions” appears to include the chance choice. Use: “Your placements and your answer may add to the representative’s bonus. Your chance choice does not.”
- “Same representative on every review…” excludes the possible switch. Use: “Same representative on every review before the final-representative choice.”
- “A different representative drawn at random from the other representatives” omits the organization filter. Use: “A different eligible representative selected at random according to your organization choice below.”
- “This choice affects only which representative your final review comes from.” It also changes the retained amount. Use: “Your slider setting affects the representative selected and how much of the $0.15 you keep, but not any representative’s bonus.”
- “You will see the selected representative’s real decision…” and “you will see the result and what you earned” are build-contingent. Verify the persisted draw, eligible unused decision, real result, and credited earnings before fielding.
- “It can add to the representative’s bonus” omits the required rate. Add: “Each point adds $0.10 if this review is picked for the representative’s bonus.”

# C. CONSISTENCY

Reviewer actions conflict: Stage 2 says “place behind the candidate,” while Stage 3 says “decide how far to follow”; change Stage 3. Representative knowledge differs: Stage 2 includes viewing and the later choice, Stage 3 only viewing; add the choice. Organization piping is consistent. For the bonus, Stage 2 omits the win gate and Stage 3’s “decisions” appears to include the chance choice; change both as in B.

# D. CLARITY FOR A HURRIED PROLIFIC READER

1. “For each one, a reviewer decides…” Rewrite: “For each referral, a reviewer places money behind the candidate. Later, the reviewer pays to adjust the chance that one final review comes from you.”
2. “Being right… makes reviewers more willing…” Rewrite: “Reviewers see each result before making later decisions.”
3. “At the end, you will make one choice…” Rewrite: “After these reviews, you will choose the source of your final review.”
4. “A different representative drawn at random…” Rewrite: “A different eligible representative is selected at random using your organization choice.”
5. “This choice affects only…” Rewrite: “The slider changes assignment and how much of the $0.15 you keep, not a representative’s bonus.”
