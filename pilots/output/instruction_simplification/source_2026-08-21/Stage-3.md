# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

In this study, you will review **three decisions made by another participant**.

We will call this participant the **representative**.

The representative took part in an earlier study and was assigned to an organization called **Atlas**.

## What the representative did

In each of their decisions, the representative reviewed two candidates:

- one candidate from **Atlas**
- one candidate from **another organization**

The representative then decided how much money to place on the Atlas candidate.

You will see **three of the representative's decisions**, each involving a different pair of candidates.

## What you will do

For each decision, you will receive your own **$0.50**.

You will decide how much of that $0.50 to place on the **same Atlas candidate**.

After each review, you will learn:

- which candidate actually scored higher
- how much you earned from that review

You will keep the money you earn from **all three reviews**.

Your three reviews can pay you **up to $3.00 in total**.

## Your decisions can also affect the representative

Your decisions may also contribute to the representative's bonus.

Whether they do depends on a random selection explained later.

This **does not affect your own earnings**. Your own payment always comes from the three reviews you complete.

---

# PAGE 2: WHAT THE REPRESENTATIVE KNEW

Before the representative made their decisions, the candidates had taken part in a separate paid study.

Each candidate completed three timed tasks:

1. **General knowledge**
2. **Word search**
3. **Logical reasoning**

Their performance affected their bonus in that study.

Candidates were assigned to organizations **at random**.

## Each decision involved two candidates

For every decision, the representative compared:

- one **Atlas candidate**
- one candidate from **another organization**

One of these candidates scored higher than the other on the **logical-reasoning task**.

There were **no ties**.

## What the representative could see

The representative could see each candidate's percentile scores on:

- general knowledge
- word search

when those scores were available.

Some scores were unavailable and were shown as **“Unknown.”**

The representative did **not** see either candidate's logical-reasoning score.

Their job was to use the information they could see to judge whether the **Atlas candidate** was the one who scored higher on logical reasoning.

## How the representative made each decision

For each decision, the representative started with **$0.50**.

They could place any amount from **$0.00 to $0.50** on the Atlas candidate.

They could only place money on the candidate from their own organization.

If the **Atlas candidate scored higher**:

- the representative kept their $0.50
- and received an additional amount equal to what they placed

If the **other candidate scored higher**:

- the amount they placed was taken out of their $0.50

Any amount they did **not** place was theirs either way.

### Example

If the representative placed **$0.20** on the Atlas candidate:

- Atlas scored higher → they received **$0.70**
- Other candidate scored higher → they received **$0.30**

So, placing more money meant backing the Atlas candidate more strongly with their own potential earnings.

The representative also knew that other participants would later see some of their decisions.

# PAGE 3: MAKING YOUR REVIEWS

You will now review **three decisions made by the same representative**.

Each review involves a different pair of candidates.

The three decisions may not appear in the same order in which the representative originally made them.

## What you will see

For each decision, you will see information about the representative:

- their study-assigned **representative ID**
- that they represented **Atlas**
- their **profile icon**

You will also see:

- the **Atlas candidate's ID**
- how much the representative placed on that candidate

## What you will not see

You will **not** see the candidate information that the representative saw.

For example, you will not see:

- either candidate's general-knowledge score
- either candidate's word-search score
- either candidate's logical-reasoning score

You will therefore make your decision based on the information shown to you about the representative and their decision.

## Your $0.50 review budget

For each review, you start with a new **$0.50**.

You decide how much of that $0.50 to place on the Atlas candidate.

You can choose any amount from **$0.00 to $0.50** using a slider from **0% to 100%**.

### If you choose 0%

You place **$0.00** on Atlas.

No matter who scored higher, you receive:

**$0.50**

### If you choose 50%

You place **$0.25** on Atlas.

- Atlas scored higher → you receive **$0.75**
- Other candidate scored higher → you receive **$0.25**

### If you choose 100%

You place the full **$0.50** on Atlas.

- Atlas scored higher → you receive **$1.00**
- Other candidate scored higher → you receive **$0.00**

## In general

Placing more on Atlas means:

- you receive **more** if the Atlas candidate scored higher
- you receive **less** if the other candidate scored higher

As you move the slider, the screen will show exactly how much you would receive under **both possible outcomes**.

## After each review

Right after you make each decision, you will learn:

- which candidate scored higher on logical reasoning
- how much you earned from that review

You keep your earnings from **all three reviews**.

For example, if your three reviews pay:

- $0.75
- $0.20
- $0.50

then you receive:

**$0.75 + $0.20 + $0.50 = $1.45**

There is **no random selection for your own review earnings**.

---

# PAGE 4: HOW YOUR DECISIONS CAN AFFECT THE REPRESENTATIVE

Your decisions can also contribute to the representative's bonus.

This is separate from your own payment.

## How the representative's bonus is selected

For the representative's bonus, the study will randomly select:

1. **one of the representative's reviewed decisions**, and
2. **one reviewer who evaluated that decision**

If you are the reviewer selected, two parts of your decisions can add to the representative's bonus.

---

## Part 1: The amount you placed

Suppose you are selected for one of the decisions you reviewed.

If the **Atlas candidate scored higher**, the representative receives an additional amount based on the percentage you placed on Atlas.

For this part of the representative's bonus, your percentage is converted to a **$1.00 scale**.

For example:

- You placed **20%** → representative receives **$0.20**
- You placed **60%** → representative receives **$0.60**
- You placed **100%** → representative receives **$1.00**

If the **other candidate scored higher**, this part adds **$0.00** to the representative's bonus.

This is different from your own payment, which uses your **$0.50 review budget**.

---

## Part 2: Your review slots

At the end of this study, you will also decide:

- how much more of this **representative's work** you would want to review
- how much more work from **Atlas representatives generally** you would want to review

You will express these decisions using **review slots**.

Each slot you give adds **$0.05** to the representative's bonus if you are the selected reviewer.

Slots count regardless of which candidate scored higher.

You can add up to **$1.00** to the representative's bonus through review slots.

---

## Maximum effect on the representative

If you are selected:

- your placement decision can add up to **$1.00**
- your review slots can add up to **$1.00**

So your decisions can add up to **$2.00** to the representative's bonus.

Again, this random selection affects **only the representative's bonus**.

Your own earnings are always the total from your **three reviews**.
