# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

Welcome to Vertex. In this study, you are a representative of Vertex, and you will complete **10 candidate reviews** on Vertex’s behalf.

In each review, you will see one candidate from Vertex and one candidate from another organization. Both candidates completed a logical-reasoning task in an earlier study, and one candidate scored higher than the other. You will **not** see their logical-reasoning scores. Instead, you will see information about their performance on two other tasks.

Your job is to judge how likely it is that the Vertex candidate scored higher on logical reasoning. You will show your judgment by deciding how much of **$0.50** to place on the Vertex candidate. You are always deciding how strongly to back Vertex, and you will **not** place money on the other candidate. The more you place, the more you earn if the Vertex candidate scored higher and the less you keep if the other candidate did; the decision screen shows the exact payoffs.

Later, independent reviewers in a separate study will see some of the decisions made by representatives like you. Your bonus will depend on **one of your reviewed decisions, chosen at random**, so any of your 10 decisions could be the one that counts.

# PAGE 2: THE CANDIDATES

The candidates participated in an earlier paid study. Each candidate completed three timed tasks: general knowledge, word search, and logical reasoning. Their performance affected their bonus in that study.

For each candidate, you will see their percentile scores on general knowledge and word search when those scores are available. A percentile tells you how the candidate performed compared with other participants in the earlier study. For example, **75th percentile** means the candidate performed better than **75%** of participants on that task.

Sometimes a score will appear as “Unknown.” This means only that the score is unavailable; it does **not** mean the candidate received a low score.

You will not see either candidate’s logical-reasoning score. In every review, one candidate scored higher on logical reasoning and one candidate scored lower, with **no ties**. Your task is to use the information you can see to judge whether the Vertex candidate was the higher scorer.

# PAGE 3: MAKING YOUR DECISIONS

Each review screen will show the Vertex candidate on the left and the other candidate on the right, along with each candidate’s available general-knowledge and word-search scores. Below the candidates, you will see a slider from **0% to 100%**. The slider determines how much of your **$0.50** you place on the Vertex candidate.

For each review, you start with **$0.50** and can place **any amount from $0.00 to $0.50** on the Vertex candidate. If the Vertex candidate scored higher, you keep your **$0.50** and receive an additional amount equal to what you placed. If the other candidate scored higher, the amount you placed is taken out of your **$0.50**. Any amount you do not place is always yours, regardless of who scored higher.

- At **0%**, you place **$0.00** on Vertex and receive **$0.50** no matter who scored higher.
- At **50%**, you place **$0.25** on Vertex. If Vertex scored higher, you receive **$0.75**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.25**.
- At **100%**, you place **$0.50** on Vertex. If Vertex scored higher, you receive **$1.00**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.00**.

The higher you move the slider, the more strongly you are backing the Vertex candidate. Placing more means that you receive more if Vertex scored higher and less if the other candidate scored higher. As you move the slider, the screen will show how much you would receive under both possible outcomes.

There is no required amount to place. Choose the amount that best reflects your judgment about the Vertex candidate.

# PAGE 4: THE INDEPENDENT REVIEW

After this study, participants in a separate study will serve as independent reviewers. Each reviewer will evaluate **three decisions** made by representatives like you, and every representative, including you, will have at least one of their decisions reviewed.

When a reviewer evaluates one of your decisions, they will see your study-assigned representative ID, that you represent Vertex, your profile icon, the Vertex candidate’s ID, and how much you placed on the Vertex candidate. The reviewer will **not** see the candidate information that you saw when making your decision. For example, they will not see the Vertex candidate’s general-knowledge or word-search score, or the other candidate’s scores. Instead, the reviewer must respond based on the information they are shown about your decision.

For each decision they review, the reviewer receives their own **$0.50** and decides how much of it to place on the same outcome: the Vertex candidate scored higher on logical reasoning. After making that decision, the reviewer learns which candidate actually scored higher.

After completing their three reviews, the reviewer also indicates how much more of your work they would want to review and how much more work from Vertex representatives generally they would want to review. These additional decisions can also affect your bonus. The next page explains exactly how your payment is calculated.

# PAGE 5: YOUR PAYMENT

You receive **$1.00** for completing the study, plus a bonus of up to **$3.00**. Your bonus will be calculated using **one of your reviewed decisions, selected at random**. If more than one reviewer evaluated that decision, **one reviewer is also selected at random**. Your bonus has three parts, each worth up to **$1.00**.

The first part is your own decision. For the selected review, your payment is calculated exactly as on the decision screen: **$0.50** plus the amount you placed if the Vertex candidate scored higher, or **$0.50** minus it if the other candidate scored higher.

The second part is the selected reviewer’s decision. If the Vertex candidate scored higher, you receive an additional amount based on the percentage the reviewer placed:

- If the reviewer places **20%**, you receive **$0.20**.
- If the reviewer places **60%**, you receive **$0.60**.
- If the reviewer places **100%**, you receive **$1.00**.

If the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.00** from this part, regardless of how much the reviewer placed. This part of your bonus can **only** add money; it can never reduce your bonus.

The third part is review slots. After completing their three reviews, the selected reviewer assigns **0 to 10 review slots** to you and **0 to 10 review slots** to Vertex, and these two numbers do not have to be the same. Each slot adds **$0.05** to your bonus. Because you represent Vertex, both the slots assigned to you and the slots assigned to Vertex count toward your bonus.

For example, suppose the reviewer assigns **6 slots** to you and **4 slots** to Vertex. That is 10 total slots:

**10 × $0.05 = $0.50**

This part of your bonus would be **$0.50**. If the reviewer assigns all 20 possible slots, this part of your bonus is **$1.00**.

---

# FULL PAYMENT EXAMPLE

Putting it together: suppose the randomly selected review is one where you placed **80% of your $0.50**, or **$0.40**, on Vertex and the Vertex candidate scored higher, making the first part **$0.50 + $0.40 = $0.90**. The selected reviewer placed **70%** on Vertex, so the second part adds **$0.70**, and assigned 6 slots to you and 4 slots to Vertex, so the 10 total slots add **10 × $0.05 = $0.50**. Your total bonus is therefore **$0.90 + $0.70 + $0.50 = $2.10**.

Because part of your bonus depends on decisions made by independent reviewers, the bonus will be paid after the reviewer study is completed.
