
# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

Welcome to Vertex.

In this study, you are a representative of **Vertex**. You will complete **10 candidate reviews** on Vertex’s behalf.

## What happens in each review

In each review, you will see two candidates:

- one candidate from **Vertex**
- one candidate from **another organization**

Both candidates completed a **logical-reasoning task** in an earlier study.

One candidate scored higher than the other, but you will **not** see their logical-reasoning scores.

Instead, you will see information about their performance on two other tasks.

## Your job

Your job is to judge:

**How likely is it that the Vertex candidate scored higher on logical reasoning?**

You will show your judgment by deciding how much of **$0.50** to place on the Vertex candidate.

You are always deciding how strongly to back **Vertex**. You will not place money on the other candidate.

## How the $0.50 works

For each review, you start with **$0.50**.

You can place **any amount from $0.00 to $0.50** on the Vertex candidate.

- If the **Vertex candidate scored higher**, you keep your $0.50 and receive an **additional amount equal to what you placed**.
- If the **other candidate scored higher**, the amount you placed is **taken out of your $0.50**.
- Any amount you **do not place is always yours**, regardless of who scored higher.

### Example

Suppose you place **$0.20** on the Vertex candidate.

- If Vertex scored higher, you receive **$0.70**.
- If the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.30**.

Placing more on Vertex means a **larger payoff if Vertex scored higher**, but a **smaller payoff if Vertex did not**.

## Why each decision matters

Later, independent reviewers in a separate study will see some of the decisions made by representatives like you.

Your bonus will depend on **one of your reviewed decisions, chosen at random**, so any of your 10 decisions could be the one that counts.

The candidates participated in an earlier paid study.

Each candidate completed three timed tasks:

1. **General knowledge**
2. **Word search**

For each candidate, you will see their percentile scores on:

- **general knowledge**
- **word search**

when those scores are available.

A percentile tells you how the candidate performed compared with other participants in the earlier study.

For example:

**75th percentile** means the candidate performed better than 75% of participants on that task.

Sometimes a score will appear as **“Unknown.”**

“Unknown” means only that the score is unavailable. It does **not** mean the candidate received a low score.

## Information you will not see

You will not see either candidate’s **logical-reasoning score**.

In every review:

- one candidate scored higher on logical reasoning,
- one candidate scored lower,
- and there are **no ties**.

Your task is to use the information you can see to judge whether the **Vertex candidate** was the higher scorer.

# PAGE 3: MAKING YOUR DECISIONS

Each review screen will show:

- the **Vertex candidate on the left**
- the **other candidate on the right**
- each candidate’s available general-knowledge and word-search scores

Below the candidates, you will see a slider from **0% to 100%**.

The slider determines how much of your **$0.50** you place on the Vertex candidate.

## What the slider means

### 0% — place $0.00

You do not place any of your $0.50 on Vertex.

No matter who scored higher, you receive:

**$0.50**

### 50% — place $0.25

You place half of your $0.50 on Vertex.

- If Vertex scored higher, you receive **$0.75**.
- If the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.25**.

### 100% — place $0.50

You place all of your $0.50 on Vertex.

- If Vertex scored higher, you receive **$1.00**.
- If the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.00**.

## In general

The higher you move the slider, the more strongly you are backing the Vertex candidate.

**Placing more on Vertex means:**

- you receive **more** if Vertex scored higher
- you receive **less** if the other candidate scored higher

As you move the slider, the screen will show how much you would receive under **both possible outcomes**.

There is no required amount to place. Choose the amount that best reflects your judgment about the Vertex candidate.

# PAGE 4: THE INDEPENDENT REVIEW

After this study, participants in a separate study will serve as **independent reviewers**.

Each reviewer will evaluate **three decisions** made by representatives like you.

Every representative, including you, will have at least one of their decisions reviewed.

## What a reviewer sees

When a reviewer evaluates one of your decisions, they will see:

- your study-assigned **representative ID**
- that you represent **Vertex**
- your **profile icon**
- the **Vertex candidate’s ID**
- how much you placed on the Vertex candidate

## What a reviewer does not see

The reviewer will **not** see the candidate information that you saw when making your decision.

For example, they will not see:

- the Vertex candidate’s general-knowledge score
- the Vertex candidate’s word-search score
- the other candidate’s scores

Instead, the reviewer must respond based on the information they are shown about your decision.

## The reviewer’s decision

For each decision they review, the reviewer receives their own **$0.50**.

They decide how much of it to place on the same outcome:

**The Vertex candidate scored higher on logical reasoning.**

After making that decision, the reviewer learns which candidate actually scored higher.

## After the three reviews

After completing their three reviews, the reviewer also indicates:

- how much more of **your work** they would want to review
- how much more work from **Vertex representatives generally** they would want to review

These additional decisions can also affect your bonus.

The next page explains exactly how your payment is calculated.

# PAGE 5: YOUR PAYMENT

You receive:

- **$1.00 for completing the study**
- plus a **bonus of up to $3.00**

Your bonus will be calculated using **one of your reviewed decisions, selected at random**.

If more than one reviewer evaluated that decision, **one reviewer is also selected at random**.

Your bonus has **three parts**.

## Part 1: Your own decision — up to $1.00

For the selected review, your payment depends on how much you placed on the Vertex candidate.

You start with **$0.50**.

- If the **Vertex candidate scored higher**, the amount you placed is **added to your $0.50**.
- If the **other candidate scored higher**, the amount you placed is **subtracted from your $0.50**.

### Example

Suppose you placed **$0.40** on Vertex.

If Vertex scored higher:

**$0.50 + $0.40 = $0.90**

If the other candidate scored higher:

**$0.50 − $0.40 = $0.10**

---

## Part 2: The reviewer’s decision — up to $1.00

The selected reviewer also decides how strongly to back the Vertex candidate.

If the **Vertex candidate scored higher**, you receive an additional amount based on the percentage the reviewer placed.

For example:

- reviewer places **20%** → you receive **$0.20**
- reviewer places **60%** → you receive **$0.60**
- reviewer places **100%** → you receive **$1.00**

If the **other candidate scored higher**, you receive **$0.00 from this part**, regardless of how much the reviewer placed.

This part of your bonus can only **add money**. It can never reduce your bonus.

---

## Part 3: Review slots — up to $1.00

After completing their three reviews, the selected reviewer assigns:

- **0 to 10 review slots to you**
- **0 to 10 review slots to Vertex**

These two numbers do not have to be the same.

Each slot adds **$0.05** to your bonus.

Because you represent Vertex, both the slots assigned **to you** and the slots assigned **to Vertex** count toward your bonus.

### Example

Suppose the reviewer assigns:

- **6 slots to you**
- **4 slots to Vertex**

That is 10 total slots.

**10 × $0.05 = $0.50**

So this part of your bonus would be **$0.50**.

If the reviewer assigns all 20 possible slots, this part of your bonus is **$1.00**.

---

# FULL PAYMENT EXAMPLE

Suppose the randomly selected review has the following results:

**Your decision**

You placed **80% of your $0.50**, or **$0.40**, on Vertex.

The Vertex candidate scored higher.

Your payment for Part 1 is:

**$0.50 + $0.40 = $0.90**

**Reviewer’s decision**

The selected reviewer placed **70%** on Vertex.

Because Vertex scored higher, Part 2 adds:

**$0.70**

**Review slots**

The reviewer assigned:

- 6 slots to you
- 4 slots to Vertex

That is 10 slots total:

**10 × $0.05 = $0.50**

## Total bonus

**$0.90 + $0.70 + $0.50 = $2.10**

Your total bonus in this example would therefore be:

**$2.10**

Because part of your bonus depends on decisions made by independent reviewers, the bonus will be paid after the reviewer study is completed.
