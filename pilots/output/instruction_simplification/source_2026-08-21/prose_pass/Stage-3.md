# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

In this study, you will review three decisions made by another participant, whom we will call the representative. The representative took part in an earlier study and was assigned to an organization called Atlas.

In each decision, the representative reviewed one candidate from Atlas and one candidate from another organization, then decided how much money to place on the Atlas candidate. You will see three of the representative’s decisions, each involving a different pair of candidates.

For each decision, you will receive your own **$0.50** and decide how much of it to place on the same Atlas candidate. After each review, you will learn which candidate actually scored higher and how much you earned from that review. You will keep the money you earn from all three reviews, which can pay you up to **$3.00** in total.

Your decisions may also contribute to the representative’s bonus, depending on a random selection explained later. This does **not** affect your own earnings. Your own payment always comes from the three reviews you complete.

---

# PAGE 2: WHAT THE REPRESENTATIVE KNEW

Before the representative made their decisions, the candidates had taken part in a separate paid study. Each candidate completed three timed tasks: general knowledge, word search, and logical reasoning. Their performance affected their bonus in that study, and candidates were assigned to organizations at random.

For every decision, the representative compared one Atlas candidate with one candidate from another organization. One of these candidates scored higher than the other on the logical-reasoning task, with **no ties**.

The representative could see each candidate’s percentile scores on general knowledge and word search when those scores were available. Some scores were unavailable and were shown as “Unknown.” The representative did **not** see either candidate’s logical-reasoning score. Their job was to use the information they could see to judge whether the Atlas candidate was the one who scored higher on logical reasoning.

For each decision, the representative started with **$0.50** and could place any amount from **$0.00 to $0.50** on the Atlas candidate. They could **only** place money on the candidate from their own organization. If the Atlas candidate scored higher, the representative kept their **$0.50** and received an additional amount equal to what they placed. If the other candidate scored higher, the amount they placed was taken out of their **$0.50**. Any amount they did not place was theirs either way.

For example, if the representative placed **$0.20** on the Atlas candidate, they received **$0.70** if Atlas scored higher and **$0.30** if the other candidate scored higher. Placing more money therefore meant backing the Atlas candidate more strongly with their own potential earnings. The representative also knew that other participants would later see some of their decisions.

---

# PAGE 3: MAKING YOUR REVIEWS

You will now review three decisions made by the same representative, each involving a different pair of candidates. The three decisions may not appear in the same order in which the representative originally made them.

For each decision, you will see the representative’s study-assigned representative ID, that they represented Atlas, and their profile icon. You will also see the Atlas candidate’s ID and how much the representative placed on that candidate. You will **not** see the candidate information that the representative saw, including either candidate’s general-knowledge, word-search, or logical-reasoning score. You will therefore make your decision based on the information shown to you about the representative and their decision.

For each review, you start with a new **$0.50** and decide how much of it to place on the Atlas candidate. You can choose any amount from **$0.00 to $0.50** using a slider from **0% to 100%**.

- At **0%**, you place **$0.00** on Atlas and receive **$0.50** no matter who scored higher.
- At **50%**, you place **$0.25** on Atlas. If Atlas scored higher, you receive **$0.75**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.25**.
- At **100%**, you place the full **$0.50** on Atlas. If Atlas scored higher, you receive **$1.00**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.00**.

Placing more on Atlas means that you receive more if the Atlas candidate scored higher and less if the other candidate scored higher. As you move the slider, the screen will show exactly how much you would receive under both possible outcomes.

Right after you make each decision, you will learn which candidate scored higher on logical reasoning and how much you earned from that review. You keep your earnings from all three reviews. For example, if your three reviews pay **$0.75**, **$0.20**, and **$0.50**, you receive:

**$0.75 + $0.20 + $0.50 = $1.45**

There is **no random selection** for your own review earnings.

---

# PAGE 4: HOW YOUR DECISIONS CAN AFFECT THE REPRESENTATIVE

Your decisions can also contribute to the representative’s bonus, separately from your own payment. For the representative’s bonus, the study will randomly select one of the representative’s reviewed decisions and one reviewer who evaluated that decision. If you are the reviewer selected, two parts of your decisions can add to the representative’s bonus.

The first part is the amount you placed. Suppose you are selected for one of the decisions you reviewed. If the Atlas candidate scored higher, the representative receives an additional amount based on the percentage you placed on Atlas. For this part of the representative’s bonus, your percentage is converted to a **$1.00 scale**:

- If you placed **20%**, the representative receives **$0.20**.
- If you placed **60%**, the representative receives **$0.60**.
- If you placed **100%**, the representative receives **$1.00**.

If the other candidate scored higher, this part adds **$0.00** to the representative’s bonus. This is different from your own payment, which uses your **$0.50 review budget**.

The second part is your review slots. At the end of this study, you will decide how much more of this representative’s work you would want to review and how much more work from Atlas representatives generally you would want to review. You will express these decisions using review slots. If you are the selected reviewer, each slot you give adds **$0.05** to the representative’s bonus, regardless of which candidate scored higher. You can add up to **$1.00** to the representative’s bonus through review slots.

If you are selected, your placement decision can add up to **$1.00** and your review slots can add up to **$1.00**, for a total of up to **$2.00**. This random selection affects **only** the representative’s bonus. Your own earnings are always the total from your three reviews.
