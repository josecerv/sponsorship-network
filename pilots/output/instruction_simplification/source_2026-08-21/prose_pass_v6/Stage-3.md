# PAGE 1: YOUR ROLE

You will review three decisions made by another participant, called the representative. The representative took part in an earlier study and was assigned to [Atlas/Vertex]. Each decision compares a different pair: one [Atlas/Vertex] candidate and one candidate from another organization.

For each review, you receive **$0.50** to place on the same [Atlas/Vertex] candidate. You then learn which candidate scored higher and how much you earned. You keep the earnings from all three reviews, up to **$3.00**.

Your decisions may also contribute to the representative’s bonus through a random selection explained later. Your own earnings always come from your three reviews.

---

# PAGE 2: WHAT THE REPRESENTATIVE KNEW

The candidates completed three timed tasks in an earlier paid study: general knowledge, word search, and logical reasoning. Their performance affected their bonus, and candidates were assigned to organizations at random. For each pair, one candidate scored higher on logical reasoning.

The representative saw each candidate’s general-knowledge and word-search percentiles when available, but not their logical-reasoning scores. A percentile compared the candidate’s performance with other participants in that study. “Unknown” meant that a score was unavailable. They used the visible information to judge whether the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher on logical reasoning.

For each decision, the representative decided how much of **$0.50** to place on the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate; they could only place money on the candidate from their own organization. If the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher, they received **$0.50** plus the amount they placed; if the other candidate scored higher, they received **$0.50** minus the amount they placed.

Placing more backed the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate more strongly with the representative’s potential earnings. The representative knew that other participants would later see some decisions.

---

# PAGE 3: MAKING YOUR REVIEWS

You will see the three decisions in an order that may differ from the order in which they were made. For each, you see the study-assigned representative ID, that they represented [Atlas/Vertex], their profile icon, the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate’s ID, and the amount placed. You do **not** see either candidate’s general-knowledge, word-search, or logical-reasoning score.

You start each review with a new **$0.50**, which works the same way as the representative’s, and a slider from **0% to 100%** sets how much of it you place on the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate.

- At **0%**, you place **$0.00** on [Atlas/Vertex] and receive **$0.50** no matter who scored higher.
- At **50%**, you place **$0.25** on [Atlas/Vertex]. If [Atlas/Vertex] scored higher, you receive **$0.75**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.25**.
- At **100%**, you place the full **$0.50** on [Atlas/Vertex]. If [Atlas/Vertex] scored higher, you receive **$1.00**; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive **$0.00**.

Place the amount that best reflects your judgment. The screen shows what you would receive under both outcomes. After each review, you learn which candidate scored higher on logical reasoning and your earnings. You keep all three amounts; there is **no random selection** for these earnings. If they are **$0.75**, **$0.20**, and **$0.50**, you receive:

**$0.75 + $0.20 + $0.50 = $1.45**

---

# PAGE 4: HOW YOUR DECISIONS CAN AFFECT THE REPRESENTATIVE

The representative gets a bonus. Part of it can come from you. The study picks one of the representative’s reviewed decisions at random. Then it picks one reviewer of that decision at random. If you are the reviewer picked, two things you did can add money to the representative’s bonus.

The first is the amount you placed. If the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher, your percentage becomes dollars for the representative:

- **20%** = **$0.20**
- **60%** = **$0.60**
- **100%** = **$1.00**

If the other candidate scored higher, this adds **$0.00**. This part uses a **$1.00 scale** for the representative. Your own pay still uses your **$0.50**.

The second comes from your answers to two short questions at the end of the study, one about this representative and one about [Atlas/Vertex] representatives in general. If you are the reviewer picked, your answers can add up to **$1.00** to the representative’s bonus, whether or not the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher.

So if you are picked, your placement can add up to **$1.00** and your answers can add up to **$1.00**, or **$2.00** in total. This only changes the representative’s bonus. Your own earnings are always the total of your three reviews.
