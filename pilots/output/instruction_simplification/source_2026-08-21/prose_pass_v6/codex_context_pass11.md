# Context for the Stage 3 review (pass 11, 2026-08-21)

## The study in one paragraph
Stage 2 participants ("representatives") are assigned to an organization ([Vertex/Atlas], piped) and make several
candidate reviews: each compares one own-organization candidate with one from another organization; they place some
of $0.50 on the own-organization candidate scoring higher on a logical-reasoning task. Stage 3 participants
("independent reviewers") each see ONE representative across three of that representative's decisions (same
representative, new candidate pair each time; the representative's profile icon is a man or a woman silhouette,
which is the experimental manipulation). For each decision the reviewer places some of their own $0.50 on the same
candidate, then learns which candidate scored higher and what they earned. Reviewers keep all three amounts.

## Payment facts that every sentence must respect (the design; all literally true)
- Reviewer's own pay: $0.50 per review, plus the amount placed if the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scored higher, minus it
  otherwise; all three reviews count; no random selection for the reviewer's own earnings; max $3.00.
- Representative's bonus from reviewers: the study picks ONE of the representative's reviewed decisions at random, then
  ONE reviewer of that decision at random. Only that reviewer's inputs count.
  (1) Placement: the picked reviewer's percentage becomes dollars on a $1.00 scale if the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate
      scored higher (20% = $0.20, 100% = $1.00), $0.00 otherwise.
  (2) Two end-of-study 0-10 items: representative-level ("how many more of this representative's decisions would you
      want to review") and organization-level ("how many more decisions by [Atlas/Vertex] representatives in general");
      each unit adds $0.05 to the representative's bonus if this reviewer is the one picked, regardless of the outcome;
      both items count; max $1.00. Combined max from one reviewer: $2.00.
- Reviewers will NOT actually review more decisions; the two items are stated preferences that also pay the
  representative. Nothing may claim or imply that the reviewer will see more decisions or candidate sets.
- Stage 2 tells representatives (Page 4, summary only): the reviewer part of their bonus depends on "how much of their
  own $0.50 they placed on the same candidate (this counts if the candidate scored higher), and how much more of your
  work they say they would want to review ... up to $2.00". Stage 3 must be consistent with that sentence.

## What the reviewer sees on the screens (the doc shows these as mockups, not text)
Review screen: representative card (profile icon, "Representative" pill, study-assigned ID, [Atlas/Vertex] badge);
candidate card ([Atlas/Vertex] candidate, ID, badge); "Amount placed by representative" bar with "82% ($0.41 of
$0.50)"; "How much of your $0.50 would you place on the [Atlas/Vertex] candidate scoring higher?" slider 0-100%;
"Amount placed: 50% ($0.25)"; two tiles "If [Atlas/Vertex] scored higher $0.75" / "If the other candidate scored
higher $0.25". Result screen: same header; "Your payment for this review $0.75"; "You placed 50%, and the Atlas
candidate scored higher. You keep this amount."; "You have 2 reviews remaining."

## Style rules for participant-facing text (the author's standing instructions)
- Prose paragraphs, 2-4 short ones per page. No sub-headers inside a page, no one-line declaratives in a row. Lists
  only where items are compared at a glance (0/50/100% examples, 20/60/100% conversion, comprehension-check options).
- Bold only on amounts, percentages, and rule words. Curly quotes only (’ “ ”). No em dashes (use comma, period,
  or parentheses; en dashes for ranges are fine). Participants never see "Page n".
- Each fact once per stage, on the page where it is needed; no "the next page explains" cross-references.
- No incentive-maximizing framing ("the more you place the more you earn" is out; "place the amount that best reflects
  your judgment" is in). Plain language for payment: short sentences, concrete conversions, "picked at random".
- Lines fully wrapped in *[brackets]* are internal design notes (rendered grey in the design doc), not participant text.
- The comprehension check on Page 5 (six items, key 1B 2A 3A 4C 5A 6A) must stay answerable from Pages 1-4.
