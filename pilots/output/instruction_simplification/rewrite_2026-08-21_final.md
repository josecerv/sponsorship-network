PAGE 1: REPRESENTING VERTEX
Welcome to Vertex. In this study, you are Vertex's representative: you will complete 10 candidate reviews on Vertex's behalf.

In each review, one Vertex candidate is paired with one candidate from a different organization. Both candidates completed a logical-reasoning task in an earlier study, and one of them scored higher than the other. You will not see those scores. What you will see are the two candidates' scores on two other tasks. Your job is to use that information to judge how likely it is that the Vertex candidate is the one who scored higher on logical reasoning.

Each review comes with its own $0.50. You show your judgment by choosing how much of that $0.50 to place on the Vertex candidate. If the Vertex candidate scored higher, you get back what you placed plus the same amount again; if the other candidate scored higher, you lose what you placed. Money you do not place is yours either way. You always back the Vertex candidate, never the other candidate. The only decision is how much.

Later, independent reviewers (participants in a separate study) will see some of your decisions, but not the candidate information you saw, and will make their own decisions about them. Your bonus will be based on one of your reviewed decisions, chosen at random, and on how one reviewer responded to it, so any of your 10 decisions could be the one that counts.

[ROW 3]
PAGE 2: THE CANDIDATES
The candidates took part in an earlier paid study. Each completed three timed tasks: general knowledge, word search, and logical reasoning. They were paid a bonus based on how well they did.

For each candidate, you will see their percentile scores on general knowledge and word search, when available. A score at the 75th percentile means the candidate did better than 75% of the other participants in that study on that task. If no score is available for a task, the profile shows "Unknown"; that is not a low score.

You will not see either candidate's logical-reasoning score. In every review, one candidate scored higher than the other on that task; there are no ties. Your payment depends on that result.

Candidates were placed into organizations at random.

Before you continue, you can open a short example of each task below.

PAGE 3: MAKING EACH DECISION
Each review screen shows your candidate (Vertex) on the left and the comparison candidate on the right, with their available scores.

Below them is a slider from 0% to 100%. It sets how much of your $0.50 review budget you place on the Vertex candidate, and you can choose any percentage from 0% to 100%. If the Vertex candidate scored higher, you receive $0.50 plus the amount you placed; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive $0.50 minus the amount you placed. As you move the slider, the screen shows both possibilities:

Place 0% ($0.00): you receive $0.50 no matter which candidate scored higher.
Place 50% ($0.25): you receive $0.75 if the Vertex candidate scored higher, $0.25 if the other candidate did.
Place 100% ($0.50): you receive $1.00 if the Vertex candidate scored higher, $0.00 if the other candidate did.

Placing more is stronger backing: more to gain if the Vertex candidate scored higher, more to lose if the other candidate did.

[ROW 7]
PAGE 5: YOUR PAYMENT
You receive $1.00 for completing the study, plus a bonus of up to $3.00.

Your bonus is based on one of your reviewed decisions, chosen at random, and on one of the reviewers who evaluated it, also chosen at random. It has three parts, each worth up to $1.00:

1. Your own decision. Your payment for the selected review: $0.50 plus the amount you placed if the Vertex candidate scored higher, $0.50 minus it if not.

2. The reviewer's decision. If the Vertex candidate scored higher in the selected review, you receive $1.00 times the percentage the selected reviewer placed (on a $1.00 scale, not the $0.50 review budget). For example, if the reviewer placed 60%, this part adds $0.60. If the other candidate scored higher, this part adds nothing, whatever the reviewer placed. This part can only add, never subtract.

3. The reviewer's review slots. After their three reviews, the reviewer says how much more of your work they would want to review, using two scores: 0 to 10 review slots for you and 0 to 10 for Vertex. These can differ: a reviewer might want more from you specifically, or more from Vertex generally. Each slot adds $0.05 to your bonus, no matter which candidate scored higher, and slots for Vertex count for you too, since you represent Vertex. For example, 6 slots for you and 4 for Vertex add $0.50; all 20 would add $1.00. Slots the reviewer does not give add nothing.

Putting it together: if you placed 80% and the Vertex candidate scored higher ($0.90), the reviewer placed 70% ($0.70), and they gave you 6 slots and Vertex 4 ($0.50), your bonus would be $2.10.

Because your bonus depends on the independent reviews, it will be paid after those reviews are complete.

PAGE 1: INDEPENDENT REVIEW
In this study, you will review three decisions made by another participant, whom we will call the representative.

In an earlier study, the representative was assigned to an organization called Atlas and reviewed candidates on Atlas's behalf. In each decision, one Atlas candidate was paired with one candidate from a different organization, and the representative placed money on the Atlas candidate. You will see three of their decisions, each about a different pair of candidates, and for each one you will get a separate $0.50 review budget and choose how much of it to place on the Atlas candidate too. After each one, you will learn how it turned out and what you earned.

Your earnings (up to $3.00) depend on your reviews and the results. If your session is selected, as explained on a later page, your reviews also affect the representative's bonus; that never changes your own earnings.

[ROW 3]
PAGE 2: THE REPRESENTATIVE'S DECISIONS
Before any of this, the candidates took part in a separate paid study. Each completed three timed tasks: general knowledge, word search, and logical reasoning. They were paid a bonus based on how well they did. Candidates were placed into organizations at random.

Each of the representative's decisions was about one pair of candidates: an Atlas candidate and a candidate from a different organization. One of the two scored higher than the other on the logical-reasoning task; there are no ties. The representative saw the two candidates' percentile scores on general knowledge and word search (when a score was available; some were missing), but not their logical-reasoning scores.

The representative had a $0.50 review budget for each decision (one of their decisions was later picked at random for payment) and chose how much of it to place on the Atlas candidate. Representatives could only place money on their own organization's candidate; the only choice was how much. Money they placed came back with the same amount again if the Atlas candidate scored higher on logical reasoning, and was lost if the other candidate did; money they did not place was theirs either way. So the amount placed shows how strongly the representative backed the Atlas candidate with their own pay. The representative also knew that reviewers would later see some of their decisions, so they were backing the candidate publicly.

[ROW 4]
PAGE 3: YOUR REVIEWS
You will see three of the representative's decisions, each about a different pair of candidates (not necessarily in the order the representative made them). For each one, you will see three things about the representative: their representative ID, that they represented Atlas, and a profile icon. You will also see the Atlas candidate's ID and the amount the representative placed, but not the candidate profiles or scores the representative saw.

For each one, you get a separate $0.50 review budget and choose how much of it to place on the Atlas candidate, using a slider from 0% to 100%. You can choose any percentage. If the Atlas candidate scored higher, you receive $0.50 plus the amount you placed; if the other candidate scored higher, you receive $0.50 minus the amount you placed. As you move the slider, the screen shows both possibilities:

Place 0% ($0.00): you receive $0.50 no matter which candidate scored higher.
Place 50% ($0.25): you receive $0.75 if the Atlas candidate scored higher, $0.25 if the other candidate did.
Place 100% ($0.50): you receive $1.00 if the Atlas candidate scored higher, $0.00 if the other candidate did.

Right after each review, you will see which candidate scored higher and what you earned. You keep your earnings from all three reviews, up to $3.00 in total.

[ROW 5]
PAGE 4: HOW YOUR REVIEWS AFFECT THE REPRESENTATIVE
The representative earns a bonus that depends partly on reviewers like you. To calculate it, we will randomly select one of the representative's reviewed decisions, and then one of the reviewers who evaluated that decision. If your session is selected, two things count, and only for that one decision:

Your amount. If the Atlas candidate scored higher in that decision, the representative receives $1.00 times the percentage you placed on it. For example, if you placed 60%, this adds $0.60 (it uses a $1.00 scale, not your $0.50). If the other candidate scored higher, it adds nothing.

Your review slots. At the end of the study, you will give review slots to this representative and to Atlas (explained then). Each slot you give adds $0.05 to the representative's bonus, no matter which candidate scored higher, up to $1.00 in total.

So if your session is selected, your amount and your slots can add up to $2.00 to the representative's bonus. This selection affects only the representative's bonus. Your own earnings are always the sum of your three reviews, with no random draw.
