Image source: Original black-and-white silhouette images retained from Stage 2. Gender-salient color treatment is applied dynamically via JavaScript at survey runtime.

Five color cues per gender:

  1. Colored border ring on avatar image
  2. Tinted background behind avatar
  3. Colored "Endorser" badge pill
  4. Colored left accent bar on endorser card
  5. Gender-colored confidence bar fill

Rationale: Color treatment makes endorser gender immediately salient without relying on participants to infer gender from silhouette shape alone. The five redundant cues ensure the manipulation is noticed even with fast reading.

Key Design Notes

Implementation details and design rationale

  • Display mapping: Raw endorsement strength |slider - 50| × 2 is mapped to the 10–90 display range via displayConfidence(raw) = round(10 + raw × 0.8). This avoids extreme values (0% or 100%) that could appear implausible.
  • Real endorser data: All endorser information is drawn from real Stage 2 responses (N = 197 endorsers, survey SV_54rmw8wULxvqS46). No fabricated endorsements are used.
  • Endorser selection: 64 unique endorsers selected (8 per cell), stratified to maximize D1 display spread within each cell while keeping all values within the target tercile.
  • D2 variance mechanism: D2 display = D1 display ± random(5..12), clamped to tercile boundaries [10–36] or [64–90]. This creates natural-looking variation between the two endorsements without changing the qualitative confidence label.
  • Comprehension checks: 5 questions presented before the decisions block. All must be answered correctly to advance. Participants who fail are looped back through the instructions.
  • Incentive scheme: $0.50 bank per decision. Participants wager 0–100% of the bank. Correct: $0.50 + $0.50 × (wager/100). Incorrect: $0.50 − $0.50 × (wager/100). Range: $0.00 to $1.00 per decision.
  • Survey deployment: Active survey SV_9Fj2oJ5lxuFUXAy on Wharton Qualtrics (yul1 datacenter). Recruitment via Prolific.

DV (Dependent Variable): Change in wager from D1 to D3, i.e., wager_D3 − wager_D1. Because D2 displays D1's endorsement strength (not D2's own), the displayed confidence is held constant across D1 and D3, yielding a clean trust-update measure that isolates the effect of outcome feedback.

Research Questions:
RQ1: Does endorser gender affect trust updating after outcome feedback?
RQ2: Does endorsement strength moderate the gender × outcome interaction?
RQ3: Do participants show asymmetric updating (penalizing failure more than rewarding success)?
